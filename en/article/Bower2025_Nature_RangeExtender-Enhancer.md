# Range extender mediates long-distance enhancer activity

## Citation (NLM)
Bower G, Hollingsworth EW, Jacinto SH, Alcantara JA, Clock B, Cao K, et al. Range extender mediates long-distance enhancer activity. Nature. 2025;643(8072):830-838. doi:10.1038/s41586-025-09221-6

**DOI:** [https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09221-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09221-6)

---

## Summary

Most mammalian enhancers regulate their target promoters over **tens of kilobases**, but a minority act over **megabase** distances. The canonical example is the ZRS enhancer controlling *Shh* in the developing limb, roughly a megabase away. What sequence features permit an enhancer to act at that range has been unknown — and the question matters, because long-range enhancers are disproportionately implicated in developmental disease when rearrangements disturb them.

The authors used **in vivo enhancer-replacement experiments at the mouse *Shh* locus**: swapping different limb enhancers into the endogenous long-range position and asking which can drive *Shh* expression from there. Replacement in the native locus, rather than reporter assays at ectopic sites, is what makes the result interpretable — the enhancer is tested in the actual genomic and chromatin context where the long-range problem exists.

The first result is a clean negative: **short- and medium-range limb enhancers cannot initiate gene expression at long-distance range.** They are perfectly functional enhancers when close to a promoter, but moving them to the megabase position abolishes activity. Range is therefore an intrinsic property of enhancer sequence, not simply a consequence of the locus architecture.

The authors then identify the missing ingredient: a *cis*-acting element they name the **range extender (REX)**, which **confers long-distance activity** on an enhancer that otherwise lacks it. Adding REX to a short-range enhancer allows it to act from the distal position. This separates enhancer function into two genetically distinct components — an activity module that determines when and where expression occurs, and a **range module** that determines over what distance that activity can be delivered. The two are separable and can be recombined.

This is a genuinely new class of regulatory element, and it changes how non-coding sequence should be annotated: an element with no enhancer activity of its own may nonetheless be essential for a distant enhancer to work.

## Significance in Introduction Context

- Introduces a **new category of cis-regulatory element** — worth emphasizing that the regulatory parts list is still incomplete
- Decomposes enhancer function into **activity** and **range**, a distinction that clarifies why some enhancers can be moved and others cannot
- Explains why **structural variants** far from a gene can be pathogenic: they may remove or displace range-conferring sequence
- The **enhancer replacement in situ** design is a model of how to test a regulatory hypothesis in native context rather than in a reporter
- Directly complements the 3D genome structural variant review and the *Shh*/limb malformation material in this vault

## Future Research Directions

- Determine how REX works mechanistically — does it promote chromatin looping, stabilize contacts, or alter local compartmentalization?
- Search genome-wide for REX-like elements, which would be invisible to standard enhancer annotation because they have no intrinsic activity
- Test whether REX elements are disrupted in human developmental disorders with unexplained long-range regulatory phenotypes
- Ask whether range extension is enhancer-agnostic — can REX confer long-range activity on any enhancer, or only compatible ones?
- Establish whether REX function is conserved across species and loci, or whether the *Shh* locus is a special case
