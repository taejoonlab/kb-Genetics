# Genome-wide association studies in a large Korean cohort identify quantitative trait loci for 36 traits and illuminate their genetic architectures

## Citation (NLM)
Jee YH, Wang Y, Jung KJ, Lee JY, Kimm H, Duan R, Price AL, Martin AR, Kraft P. Genome-wide association studies in a large Korean cohort identify quantitative trait loci for 36 traits and illuminate their genetic architectures. Nat Commun. 2025;16:4560. doi:10.1038/s41467-025-59950-5

**DOI:** [https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-59950-5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-59950-5)

---

## Summary

GWAS has been overwhelmingly conducted in European-ancestry populations, which restricts both biological discovery and the clinical applicability of findings elsewhere. This study reports GWAS across **36 quantitative traits in 153,950 individuals** from the **Korean Cancer Prevention Study-II (KCPS2) Biobank** — a large East Asian cohort with the standard clinical measurements (anthropometric, lipid, liver, kidney, haematological, and endocrine traits) that make quantitative-trait GWAS powerful.

Analysing KCPS2 alone, the authors discovered **301 previously unreported genetic loci**, including an association between **thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) and *CD36***. Loci found in a single new cohort of this size are a direct measure of how much signal remains inaccessible when a population is not studied.

They then performed **meta-analysis with the Korean Genome and Epidemiology Study, Biobank Japan, Taiwan Biobank, and UK Biobank**. This combination identified **4,588 loci that were not significant in any single contributing GWAS** — a striking demonstration that meta-analysis is not merely additive. Variants with modest effects that fall below significance everywhere individually can cross the threshold when evidence is pooled, and this works across ancestries as well as within them.

Beyond discovery, the paper characterizes **differences in genetic architecture between East Asian and European samples**. Effect sizes, allele frequencies, and linkage disequilibrium structure differ, so the same underlying biology can present differently in a GWAS depending on the population studied. The authors also highlight **East Asian-specific associations** — signals driven by variants that are common in East Asian populations and rare or absent in Europeans, and therefore undiscoverable in European-only studies regardless of sample size. The best-known example of this class is the *ALDH2* variant affecting alcohol metabolism; such variants are not rare curiosities but a systematic category of biology that Eurocentric GWAS cannot reach.

## Significance in Introduction Context

- Directly relevant to a Korean genetics course: this is the genetic architecture of the students' own population, measured at biobank scale
- Demonstrates that **ancestry-specific variants** exist as a systematic category — some biology is simply invisible in European samples
- Shows the power of **meta-analysis** quantitatively: 4,588 loci that no contributing study could find alone
- Explains why **genetic architecture is population-dependent** through allele frequency and LD differences, not different underlying biology
- Complements the Pan-UK Biobank study and the GenomeIndia comment in this vault — three approaches to the same representation problem

## Future Research Directions

- Fine-map the 301 novel loci to causal variants, exploiting the different LD structure in East Asian populations to narrow intervals that European data cannot resolve
- Determine the mechanism linking *CD36* to TSH, which is not an obvious connection and may reveal new thyroid biology
- Develop and evaluate polygenic scores trained in Korean data and test their performance against European-derived scores in Korean individuals
- Extend the trait set beyond the 36 quantitative measures to disease endpoints within the same cohort
- Investigate whether East Asian-specific associations reflect recent selection, drift, or ascertainment, using population-genetic analysis of the implicated variants
