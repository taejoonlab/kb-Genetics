# Dated gene duplications elucidate the evolutionary assembly of eukaryotes

## Citation (NLM)
Kay CJ, Spang A, Szöllősi GJ, Pisani D, Williams TA, Donoghue PCJ. Dated gene duplications elucidate the evolutionary assembly of eukaryotes. Nature. 2026;650(8091):129-137. doi:10.1038/s41586-025-09808-z

**DOI:** [https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09808-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09808-z)

---

## Summary

The origin of eukaryotes was a formative but poorly understood event. Competing hypotheses of **eukaryogenesis** differ principally in **the timing of mitochondrial endosymbiosis relative to the acquisition of other eukaryotic novelties** — the nucleus, endomembrane system, cytoskeleton, phagocytosis, and sexual reproduction. "Mito-early" scenarios hold that the endosymbiont arrived at a relatively simple archaeal host and drove subsequent complexity; "mito-late" scenarios hold that the host had already evolved substantial eukaryotic machinery, including phagocytosis, before engulfing the bacterium. Discriminating between them has been extremely difficult because **no living lineage represents an intermediate step** — every eukaryote alive today descends from the last eukaryotic common ancestor (LECA), which already had mitochondria.

The authors exploit a source of temporal information that does not require intermediates. Many eukaryotic cell functions depend on genes that arose by **duplication during eukaryogenesis** — for example, the paralogue families underlying vesicle trafficking, the cytoskeleton, and nuclear transport. Each duplication is an event with a position in time. If duplications can be **dated**, the sequence in which eukaryotic systems were assembled can be reconstructed, and the position of mitochondrial acquisition within that sequence read off directly.

Using a **relaxed molecular clock** applied to dated duplication events across eukaryotic gene families, the authors reconstruct the temporal order of eukaryogenesis. The approach is powerful because it converts a phylogenetic question, where topology alone cannot resolve timing, into a chronological one, where the relative order of internal events is estimated explicitly.

The result places the assembly of eukaryotic cellular systems on a timeline relative to endosymbiosis, addressing the central point of disagreement between competing scenarios rather than adding more comparative data to the existing debate.

## Significance in Introduction Context

- Addresses one of the largest questions in evolutionary biology — **where eukaryotes came from** — with an explicitly genetic method
- Demonstrates that **gene duplication** is not only a source of new genes but a **dateable historical record** of cellular evolution
- Explains the **molecular clock** and relaxed-clock dating in a context where the answer genuinely depends on the method
- Illustrates how to reason about evolutionary events with **no surviving intermediates**
- Should be read alongside the Tobiasson and Bernabeu papers in this vault, which reach the same question from gene-origin rather than gene-duplication evidence

## Future Research Directions

- Test the robustness of the inferred order to clock model, calibration choice, and gene family sampling, since dating is assumption-sensitive
- Extend dating to additional paralogue families to check whether independent systems give a consistent timeline
- Reconcile the duplication-based chronology with gene-origin analyses of the LECA proteome, which address the same question differently
- Incorporate newly discovered Asgard archaeal lineages, which continue to change what the host is thought to have possessed
- Ask whether the reconstructed order is consistent with the cell-biological feasibility of each intermediate state
