# Mobile integrons encode phage defense systems

## Citation (NLM)
Kieffer N, Hipólito A, Ortiz-Miravalles L, Blanco P, Delobelle T, Vizuete P, et al. Mobile integrons encode phage defense systems. Science. 2025;388(6747):606. doi:10.1126/science.ads0915

**DOI:** [https://doi.org/10.1126/science.ads0915](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.ads0915)

---

## Summary

**Integrons** are genetic elements that promote bacterial adaptation to changing environments. They are **recombination platforms** that capture **gene cassettes** and store them in arrays of functionally unrelated adaptive genes. Expression of the array depends on a single promoter, **Pc**, located in the platform, and is modulated by **polar effects**: cassettes near Pc are expressed strongly, and those further along the array progressively less. Under stress, integrons can **reshuffle cassettes**, changing which are near the promoter and therefore which are expressed. The system is effectively a bacterial memory of adaptive functions with a reorderable priority queue.

Integrons are best known clinically as the vehicles of **multidrug antibiotic resistance**, and the resistance cassettes they carry have dominated study of these elements. But those cassettes are a recent, human-driven addition. Most integron cassettes in nature encode proteins of **unknown function**, and what integrons were doing before antibiotics is largely unresolved.

This study answers part of that question: **mobile integrons encode phage defense systems**. Cassettes of previously unknown function turn out to be **anti-phage defences**, placing bacteriophage predation — the dominant and constant selective pressure on bacteria — among the forces that shaped integron content long before clinical antibiotics existed.

The integron architecture is unusually well suited to this role. Phage pressure is variable and specific: which defence is useful depends on which phages are present, and defence systems are typically costly or toxic when expressed constitutively. Cassette reshuffling under stress allows a cell to promote a relevant defence to the highly expressed position near Pc while keeping others in storage — a mechanism for **regulating defence by physical reordering** rather than by transcriptional control alone.

## Significance in Introduction Context

- Introduces **integrons** as a distinct mechanism of bacterial genome plasticity, separate from plasmids and transposons
- The **positional expression** logic — where a gene sits determines how much it is expressed — is an unusual and memorable regulatory mechanism
- Reframes a clinically infamous element as fundamentally a **phage defence system**, correcting an anthropocentric view shaped by antibiotic-era sampling
- Reinforces bacteriophage predation as the dominant selective pressure on bacteria
- Complements the CRISIS anti-phage regulation paper in this vault: two different solutions to the same problem of managing costly defence systems

## Future Research Directions

- Determine what fraction of the unknown-function cassette repertoire encodes defence, and what the remainder does
- Test whether cassette reshuffling is actually triggered by phage infection, which would establish the regulatory model directly
- Measure the fitness cost of each defence cassette when highly expressed, to confirm why storage in low-expression positions is advantageous
- Ask whether integron-encoded defences differ functionally from chromosomally encoded ones, given that mobility allows horizontal spread
- Investigate whether phage defence and antibiotic resistance cassettes compete for the high-expression positions near Pc
