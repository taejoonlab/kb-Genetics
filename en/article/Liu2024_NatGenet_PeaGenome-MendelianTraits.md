# Reference genome sequence and population genomic analysis of peas provide insights into the genetic basis of Mendelian and other agronomic traits

## Citation (NLM)
Liu N, Lyu X, Zhang X, Zhang G, Zhang Z, Guan X, et al. Reference genome sequence and population genomic analysis of peas provide insights into the genetic basis of Mendelian and other agronomic traits. Nat Genet. 2024;56(9):1964-1974. doi:10.1038/s41588-024-01867-8

**DOI:** [https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-024-01867-8](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-024-01867-8)

---

## Summary

Peas (*Pisum sativum*, 2n = 14) are the plant on which Gregor Mendel worked out the laws of inheritance, and they remain the fourth most produced legume globally after soybean, common bean, and chickpea. Domesticated for more than 10,000 years, with the earliest archaeological finds from the late Neolithic Mediterranean and Near East, pea carries extensive genetic diversity. This study provides the modern genomic resource for the species and — for a genetics course, the essential part — **identifies the actual genes behind several of Mendel's seven trait pairs**.

The authors assembled the genome of the elite vegetable pea cultivar **'Zhewan No. 1'** at chromosome level. The pea genome is large and repeat-rich: long terminal repeat retrotransposons occupy 23.14% of the assembly (45.34% Gypsy-like, 11.48% Copia-like), which is why a good pea assembly was slow to arrive. They then resequenced **314 accessions** to build a comprehensive map of genetic variation, and performed genome-wide association studies identifying **235 candidate loci associated with 57 agronomic traits**.

The centrepiece is the molecular identification of Mendel's traits. The authors pinpointed the **causal gene haplotypes for four Mendelian traits**: **stem length (*Le/le*)** — tall versus dwarf; **flower colour (*A/a*)** — purple versus white; **cotyledon colour (*I/i*)** — yellow versus green; and **seed shape (*R/r*)** — round versus wrinkled. They additionally identified the genes controlling **pod form (Mendel's *P/p*)** and hilum colour. Reaching back 160 years to attach molecular identities to the specific alleles Mendel scored in his garden is a rare kind of scientific closure, and it also demonstrates that his traits were genuinely simple: each is a discrete, largely single-locus character, which is precisely why his crosses gave clean ratios.

Finally, the authors constructed a **gene expression atlas across 22 tissues**, highlighting gene modules for pod and seed development. Together the assembly, variation map, GWAS results, and expression atlas provide the resource base for genome-informed pea breeding.

## Significance in Introduction Context

- The perfect companion to the Mendel lecture: the same traits, the same species, now with the genes named — students can see the molecular basis of *Le/le*, *A/a*, *I/i*, *R/r*
- Explains **why Mendel's traits behaved so cleanly**: they really are discrete single-locus characters, which is a fortunate and non-representative feature of his choices
- Contrasts sharply with the 57 agronomic traits mapped to 235 loci in the same study — most pea traits are polygenic, so the same paper contains both the Mendelian and the quantitative genetic worlds
- Demonstrates the standard modern crop genomics pipeline: reference assembly → population resequencing → GWAS → candidate gene → expression atlas
- Connects to the "Beyond Mendel" perspective in this vault, which argues we generalize too readily from Mendel's atypically simple traits

## Key References

1. **Mendel (1866)** — *Versuche über Pflanzen-Hybriden* — The original experiments whose traits are here resolved molecularly
2. **Bhattacharyya et al. (1990)** — The wrinkled-seed character of pea is caused by a transposon-like insertion in a gene encoding starch-branching enzyme — The first of Mendel's traits solved molecularly (*R/r*)
3. **Martin et al. (1997); Lester et al. (1997)** — *Le* encodes gibberellin 3β-hydroxylase — Molecular basis of stem length
4. **Hellens et al. (2010)** — Identification of Mendel's white flower character (*A* encodes a bHLH transcription factor) — Flower colour resolved
5. **Armstead et al. (2007); Sato et al. (2007)** — *I* locus and stay-green / *SGR* — Cotyledon colour
6. **Kreplak et al. (2019)** — A reference genome for pea (Caméor) — The prior pea assembly this work extends
7. **FAO production statistics** — Global legume production — Source of the agronomic context

## Future Research Directions

- Functionally validate the newly assigned causal haplotypes for pod form and hilum colour by transformation or editing
- Use the 314-accession variation map to reconstruct pea domestication history and identify selective sweeps
- Determine why some Mendelian loci show multiple independent causal haplotypes, which speaks to how often each phenotype arose during domestication
- Apply the GWAS results and expression atlas to breeding for pod and seed quality traits in vegetable pea
- Compare Mendel's loci across legumes to ask whether the same genes control the analogous traits in bean, chickpea, and lentil
