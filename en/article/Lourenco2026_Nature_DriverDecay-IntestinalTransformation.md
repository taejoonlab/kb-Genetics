# Decay of driver mutations shapes the landscape of intestinal transformation

## Citation (NLM)
Lourenço FC, Sadien ID, Wong K, Adler S, Sawle A, Schubert Santana L, et al. Decay of driver mutations shapes the landscape of intestinal transformation. Nature. 2026;649(8087):729-738. doi:10.1038/s41586-025-09762-w

**DOI:** [https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09762-w](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09762-w)

---

## Summary

Colorectal cancer (CRC) has traditionally been described by the **stepwise model**: sequential mutation of *APC*, then *KRAS*, *TP53* and others, with each mutation driving the expansion of a positively selected clone — the classic Fearon–Vogelstein adenoma–carcinoma sequence. Recent work complicates this picture. Many premalignant lesions turn out to be **polyclonal**, comprising multiple clones expressing **different mutant *APC* proteins**. If a lesion contains several independently initiated *APC*-mutant clones, then initiation was not a single rare event followed by orderly succession.

The authors investigated this by **mediating transformation on different mouse genetic backgrounds** carrying mutations in *Kras* or other common CRC drivers — that is, systematically varying the pre-existing driver context and asking how it changes what transformation produces. This is a controlled version of the natural situation in which normal intestinal epithelium already carries scattered driver mutations before any lesion forms.

Their central concept is **decay of driver mutations**. A driver mutation's selective advantage is not a fixed property of the mutation; it depends on the state of the cell and tissue it arises in. As **priming events** accumulate in normal epithelium, the marginal advantage conferred by a subsequent driver **decays** — a mutation that would be strongly advantageous in a naive background provides much less benefit in a background that already carries a related driver. Selection coefficients are therefore context-dependent and change as the tissue's mutational landscape fills in.

This reframes the stepwise model. The order and combination of drivers observed in tumours reflects not a fixed pathway but a **shifting selective landscape** in which each acquired driver alters the value of the next. Diverse priming events in normal epithelium mean different fields have different selective topographies, which shapes which transformations are possible where.

## Significance in Introduction Context

- Revises the **multi-step model of carcinogenesis** taught in every cancer genetics lecture, replacing a fixed sequence with a context-dependent landscape
- Makes **epistasis** concrete in a clinical setting: the effect of a driver depends on the drivers already present
- Explains **polyclonal premalignant lesions**, which the classical single-clone model cannot accommodate
- Demonstrates the value of **defined mouse genetic backgrounds** for testing selection, which cannot be done in human tissue
- Complements the NF1 second-hit study and the Calvet bladder selection paper in this vault — all concern selection in normal or premalignant tissue

## Future Research Directions

- Quantify driver decay explicitly: measure selection coefficients for the same driver across defined background genotypes
- Determine whether decay predicts which driver combinations are observed in human tumours and which are absent
- Test whether therapeutic vulnerability depends on the order in which drivers were acquired, not just which are present
- Map the primed landscape of normal human intestine to predict where transformation is most likely
- Ask whether driver decay operates in other epithelia with high somatic mutation burden, such as oesophagus and skin
