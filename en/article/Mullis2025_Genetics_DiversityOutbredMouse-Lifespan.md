# Analysis of lifespan across diversity outbred mouse studies identifies multiple longevity-associated loci

## Citation (NLM)
Mullis MN, Wright KM, Raj A, Gatti DM, Reifsnyder PC, Flurkey K, et al. Analysis of lifespan across diversity outbred mouse studies identifies multiple longevity-associated loci. Genetics. 2025;230(4):iyaf081. doi:10.1093/genetics/iyaf081

**DOI:** [https://doi.org/10.1093/genetics/iyaf081](https://doi.org/10.1093/genetics/iyaf081)

---

## Summary

Lifespan is the ultimate **integrative phenotype** — it summarizes health across every physiological system. That makes it both attractive and difficult. Difficult, because hundreds of factors each contribute a little, so statistical power to find any one of them is low, and because a locus that shifts lifespan could be acting through any unknown subsystem. Attractive, because a factor that does emerge from that noise is likely to have unusually broad impact on health.

In humans, the **heritability of lifespan is low** — consistently under 20%, and under 10% once assortative mating is properly accounted for (Ruby et al. 2018). That ceiling severely limits QTL discovery, and human longevity loci often fail to replicate across populations and study designs. Outbred mouse populations offer an orthogonal approach with a clear trade-off. The disadvantage is sample size: mouse experiments run in the dozens to hundreds, versus hundreds of thousands in human biobanks. The advantages are substantial: **well-balanced allele frequencies** (which raise power per animal), essentially **no population structure** to confound genotype–phenotype association, the ability to enrol animals young and follow them to natural death (a true prospective design), and the ability to **impose treatments and environmental perturbations** and measure their interaction with genotype.

The authors performed a genetic **mega-analysis** — pooling and jointly analyzing raw data rather than summary statistics — of longevity in **Diversity Outbred (DO)** mice. DO mice are a heterogeneous stock derived from eight founder strains, recombined over many generations, so each animal is a unique mosaic of the eight founder haplotypes with high mapping resolution and balanced allele frequencies. The dataset comprised **2,444 animals from three independently conducted lifespan studies**, two of which were previously unpublished, and included diet and drug interventions.

They identified **8 loci** that significantly affected lifespan **independently of diet and drug treatment** in at least one study. One of these acted in a **sex-dependent** manner. In addition, they detected a further locus with a **diet-specific** effect — a genotype-by-environment interaction on lifespan, detectable only because the design deliberately varied the environment. Collectively, the identified loci explained **over half of the estimated heritable variation in lifespan** across these studies. That is a striking figure relative to human longevity GWAS and reflects exactly the design advantages listed above: balanced allele frequencies, no structure, controlled environments, and prospective follow-up to death.

## Significance in Introduction Context

- A clean, current worked example of **QTL mapping for a complex quantitative trait** in a designed mapping population — the concrete counterpart to the theory in the complex-traits lecture
- Explains why **heritability** sets a ceiling on discovery, and why a low-heritability trait is hard to map even with enormous samples
- Introduces **multiparental populations** (Diversity Outbred, Collaborative Cross) and why they beat two-parent crosses: many founder haplotypes, fine recombination, balanced allele frequencies
- Demonstrates **genotype-by-environment interaction** as an empirical result, not an abstraction — one locus only matters on a particular diet
- Contrasts model-organism and human genetics honestly: mice trade sample size for design control, and here design control wins

## Future Research Directions

- Resolve the eight loci to causal genes and variants using founder-haplotype effects, expression QTL data, and targeted validation
- Determine the physiological pathway each locus acts through — is a longevity locus acting on cancer incidence, metabolic health, immune function, or something else?
- Investigate the sex-dependent locus mechanistically: is the interaction hormonal, chromosomal, or driven by sex-biased gene expression?
- Extend the diet-specific finding into a systematic G×E design across multiple diets and interventions to map the interaction landscape rather than single interactions
- Test whether orthologous human loci show association with longevity in biobank data, using the mouse result as a prior to boost power
