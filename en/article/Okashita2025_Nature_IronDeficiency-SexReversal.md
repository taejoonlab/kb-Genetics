# Maternal iron deficiency causes male-to-female sex reversal in mouse embryos

## Citation (NLM)
Okashita N, Maeda R, Kuroki S, Sasaki K, Uno Y, Koopman P, Tachibana M. Maternal iron deficiency causes male-to-female sex reversal in mouse embryos. Nature. 2025;643(8070):262-270. doi:10.1038/s41586-025-09063-2

**DOI:** [https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09063-2](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09063-2)

---

## Summary

Mammalian sex determination is presented as a paradigm of genetic determinism: the Y-linked gene ***Sry*** is expressed in the bipotential gonad during a narrow developmental window, initiates the testis pathway, and an XY embryo becomes male. This paper shows the pathway is nutritionally vulnerable, and that a **maternal dietary deficiency can override a chromosomal male genotype**.

The mechanistic link runs through **iron**. Ferrous iron (Fe²⁺) is an essential cofactor for a large class of oxidoreductases, including the **2-oxoglutarate-dependent dioxygenases** that demethylate DNA and histones. Normal epigenetic regulation of *Sry* requires **histone demethylation** — specifically, removal of the repressive H3K9 methylation mark by **KDM3A** — so that *Sry* can be expressed at the right moment. Iron is therefore not a general nutritional background but a direct chemical requirement of the enzyme that licenses *Sry* expression.

The authors first showed that **Fe²⁺-producing pathways are substantially activated in mouse embryonic gonads during the sex-determining period**, indicating the gonad actively raises its iron availability precisely when demethylation is required. They then tested the requirement directly: **chelating iron in cultured XY gonads reduced KDM3A-mediated H3K9 demethylation at *Sry*, largely abolished *Sry* expression, and caused the gonads to express ovarian markers.** The chain runs from iron availability to histone demethylase activity to *Sry* transcription to gonadal fate.

Extending this to the whole animal, **maternal iron deficiency caused male-to-female sex reversal in mouse embryos** — an XY embryo developing as female because its mother's diet did not supply enough iron at the critical window.

The result reframes a canonical example. *Sry* remains the switch, but whether the switch can be thrown depends on a metabolic input supplied by the mother, making sex determination an instance of gene–environment interaction rather than pure genetic determination.

## Significance in Introduction Context

- Directly complicates the sex-determination lecture's cleanest story: **chromosomal sex does not guarantee phenotypic sex** when a required cofactor is limiting
- A concrete, mechanistically complete example of **gene–environment interaction** — not a statistical interaction term but a named enzyme missing a named cofactor
- Connects **metabolism to epigenetics**: histone and DNA demethylases require Fe²⁺ and 2-oxoglutarate, so nutritional state feeds directly into chromatin state
- Illustrates **maternal effects** in mammals, where the mother's physiology determines an offspring phenotype not encoded in the offspring's genome
- Relevant to human health, since iron deficiency is among the most common nutritional deficiencies worldwide

## Future Research Directions

- Determine whether human sex development is similarly sensitive to maternal iron status, which would have immediate public health implications
- Establish the threshold and timing: how severe must deficiency be, and how narrow is the vulnerable window?
- Test whether other Fe²⁺-dependent demethylase targets in the embryo are affected simultaneously, since KDM3A is unlikely to be uniquely sensitive
- Ask whether this mechanism contributes to sex ratio variation in wild mammal populations under nutritional stress
- Investigate whether other 2-oxoglutarate-dependent dioxygenase cofactors, such as oxygen or ascorbate, produce comparable effects on sex determination
