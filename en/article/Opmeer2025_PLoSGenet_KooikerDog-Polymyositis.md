# Polymyositis in Kooiker dogs is associated with a 39 kb deletion upstream of the canine IL21/IL2 locus

## Citation (NLM)
Opmeer Y, van Steenbeek FG, Rozendom C, Fieten H, Diaz Espineira MM, Stassen QEM, et al. Polymyositis in Kooiker dogs is associated with a 39 kb deletion upstream of the canine IL21/IL2 locus. PLoS Genet. 2025;21(1):e1011538. doi:10.1371/journal.pgen.1011538

**DOI:** [https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1011538](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1011538)

---

## Summary

Polymyositis in the Dutch Kooiker dog is an autoimmune inflammatory disease of skeletal muscle. Affected dogs present with swallowing and locomotion difficulties, and the outcome is generally poor. Because affected dogs were frequently closely related, an inherited cause was suspected. This paper reports the molecular genetic investigation, and it is an unusually complete example of a modern mapping study — from association, through haplotype, to a specific structural variant, to a functional consequence, to a quantitative penetrance estimate.

A **genome-wide association study** of 33 cases and 106 controls implicated a region on chromosome **CFA19** with p = 4.7 × 10⁻¹⁰. Note how small this study is: 139 dogs would be hopeless in a human GWAS. Dog breeds are effectively closed populations with long haplotype blocks and few segregating variants of large effect, which is precisely why canine genetics can map traits with sample sizes two or three orders of magnitude smaller than human studies. **Haplotype analysis** showed the cases shared a **2.9 Mb region**, homozygous in some dogs and heterozygous in others.

**Whole-genome sequencing** then localized the causal candidate to a **39 kb deletion** situated **10 kb upstream of the neighbouring interleukin genes *IL21* and *IL2***. Both encode key regulators of the cellular immune response, which fits the autoimmune phenotype. The deletion allele frequency was **0.81 among cases** and **0.25 in a random sample of the breed** — high in the breed generally, which is why the disease persists.

The functional test is the strongest part. Leukocytes from **affected, untreated dogs homozygous for the deletion overexpressed *IL21* and *IL2* upon mitogen stimulation**. The deleted interval therefore appears to contain **negative regulatory elements**; removing them de-represses the cytokines. This is a **non-coding, regulatory** variant — the protein sequences are intact, but their control is broken.

Finally, the authors estimate **penetrance**, assuming causality: about **10–20% for homozygotes** and **0.5–2% for heterozygotes** over a lifetime. This is a risk allele, not a deterministic Mendelian mutation — most homozygous dogs never develop polymyositis. The authors note the human relevance: the **IL21/IL2 chromosomal region** is already associated with several human autoimmune diseases, and this result suggests that distant upstream regulatory variants, not just coding changes, deserve attention there too.

## Significance in Introduction Context

- A complete worked example of the modern mapping pipeline: **GWAS → haplotype sharing → WGS → structural variant → functional assay → penetrance estimate**
- Explains concretely why **dogs are a powerful genetic model**: breed structure, long haplotypes, and enriched risk alleles let 139 animals achieve what would need tens of thousands of humans
- **Incomplete penetrance** made numerical — 10–20% for homozygotes — a far more useful lesson than the abstract definition
- The causal variant is a **regulatory deletion**, not a coding mutation, illustrating that most complex-disease variants lie outside genes
- Supports both the dog model-organism lecture and the human disease/GWAS lectures, and shows how veterinary genetics feeds back into human medicine

## Future Research Directions

- Identify which specific elements within the 39 kb interval are the repressors — chromatin accessibility, enhancer/silencer reporter assays, and 3D contact mapping to *IL21*/*IL2*
- Determine why penetrance is only 10–20%: search for modifier loci, MHC/DLA haplotype effects, and environmental or infectious triggers
- Test whether the deletion affects *IL21*, *IL2*, or both, and in which immune cell types
- Use the deletion as a breeding marker to reduce disease incidence while monitoring loss of genetic diversity in a small breed
- Look for orthologous non-coding deletions or regulatory variants upstream of human *IL21*/*IL2* in autoimmune disease cohorts
