# Passenger mutations link cellular origin and transcriptional identity in human lung adenocarcinomas

## Citation (NLM)
Panja S, Mantri P, Johnson KE, Andrade-Martinez JS, Yang SR, Deshpande A, et al. Passenger mutations link cellular origin and transcriptional identity in human lung adenocarcinomas. Nat Genet. 2025;57(12):3066-3074. doi:10.1038/s41588-025-02418-5

**DOI:** [https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-025-02418-5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-025-02418-5)

---

## Summary

The **cell of origin (COO)** — the normal cell type a cancer arose from — is fundamental to understanding carcinogenesis, but inferring it in human tumours is difficult. Conventional inference relies on resemblance: non-small cell lung cancer is divided into **LUAD** (resembling glandular epithelium of the **distal** airway, i.e. alveolar type 2 cells) and **LUSC** (resembling stratified epithelium of **proximal** bronchi, i.e. basal cells), and mouse models confirm that transforming AT2 and basal cells produces LUAD and LUSC respectively. But resemblance is unreliable, because tumours show **lineage plasticity**: identity changes after treatment, and 80% of untreated LUADs already display two or more distinct histological patterns within one tumour. A cell that no longer resembles its origin cannot be traced by resemblance.

The authors exploit an entirely different signal. **DNA damage is preferentially repaired in transcribed genes** (transcription-coupled repair), so the regional density of somatic mutations across the genome is depressed where the cell was transcribing. Critically, this imprint is laid down by the transcriptional program of the **cell that was mutating** — the normal ancestor — not by the tumour's current expression state. **Passenger mutations**, which are functionally inert and therefore not selected, preserve this record faithfully. They are a molecular fossil of the cell of origin.

In aggregate, the prediction held: **LUAD mutation density correlated most strongly with distal (alveolar) cell-type-specific expression, and LUSC with proximal (basal) expression**, matching the presumed origins. Applying the method to individual genomes then produced the surprise: **21% of LUADs carry mutational footprints of proximal airway origin**, with a further **38% ambiguous**. A substantial minority of tumours classified as adenocarcinoma did not arise from the cell type their histology implies.

These origin classes have distinct biology. **Distal-origin LUADs** are enriched for ***KRAS*** and ***STK11*** drivers and occur mainly in **smokers**. **Proximal-origin LUADs** are enriched for ***EGFR*** drivers and are more common in **never-smokers** — which is striking, because EGFR-mutant, never-smoker LUAD is already recognized clinically as a distinct entity, and this offers a developmental explanation for it. **Ambiguous-origin LUADs** show **APOBEC mutational signatures** and ***SMARCA4*** alterations. Finally, ***TP53*-mutant LUADs with non-distal origins preferentially display non-distal transcriptional identity**, indicating an interplay in which loss of p53 permits the tumour's expression program to remain closer to its true lineage.

## Significance in Introduction Context

- Turns **passenger mutations** — usually dismissed as noise — into an information source, an elegant reversal worth highlighting
- Explains **transcription-coupled repair** and its genome-wide consequence: mutation density is a readout of past transcription
- Distinguishes **lineage (where a cell came from)** from **identity (what it currently expresses)**, and shows they can dissociate
- Connects a molecular inference directly to clinical categories: smoking status, *EGFR* versus *KRAS* driver, and treatment-relevant subgroups
- A good example of inferring history from data that were not designed to record it — the same logic as phylogenetics or coalescent inference

## Future Research Directions

- Validate the origin assignments experimentally, for example by comparing mutational-footprint classification with single-cell transcriptional lineage evidence in the same tumours
- Test whether origin class predicts response to targeted therapy or immunotherapy independently of driver mutation status
- Determine why ambiguous-origin tumours carry APOBEC signatures and *SMARCA4* alterations — is chromatin remodelling erasing the footprint, or is the origin genuinely intermediate?
- Apply the passenger-mutation approach to other cancers with contested cells of origin, particularly those where histology is unreliable
- Investigate the *TP53*–identity relationship mechanistically: does p53 loss stabilize or destabilize lineage identity?
