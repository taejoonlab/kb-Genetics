# Chemical Genomics in Escherichia coli Identifies an Inhibitor of Bacterial Lipoprotein Targeting

## Citation (NLM)
Pathania R, Zlitni S, Barker C, Das R, Gerritsma DA, Lebert J, Awuah E, Melacini G, Capretta FA, Brown ED. Chemical genomics in Escherichia coli identifies an inhibitor of bacterial lipoprotein targeting. Nat Chem Biol. 2009;5(11):849-856. doi:10.1038/nchembio.221

**DOI:** [https://doi.org/10.1038/nchembio.221](https://doi.org/10.1038/nchembio.221)

---

## Summary

Understanding the mechanism of action of hits from cell-based screens is a major bottleneck in antibacterial discovery; only two new antibiotic classes (linezolid, daptomycin) reached the clinic in about 40 years. Yeast chemical genomics uses heterozygous deletion pools, but this is not possible in monoploid bacteria. The authors instead use high-copy suppression: overexpressing a drug's target can titrate the compound and rescue growth. They assembled an ordered, high-expression array of essential *E. coli* genes (from the ASKA clone library; clones patched at 384 density on agar) and screened, rather than selected, for suppressors at increasing multiples of the minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC).

The compound library of about 50,000 molecules was screened at 50 uM against an LPS-defective, hypersensitive strain (MC1061). Among 14 antibiotics tested (six reported), the known targets emerged as high-copy suppressors at 16x MIC where a single gene encodes the target: fosfomycin-murA, fosmidomycin-dxr, trimethoprim-folA, sulfamethoxazole-folC and D-cycloserine-ddl. (The second D-cycloserine target, DadX, is nonessential and absent from the array.) Spectinomycin showed few interactions and promiscuous suppressors such as yjeE, rpsR, rpmB and tolC (an efflux channel) appeared at lower stringency. No two antibiotics had the same suppressor profile, i.e., a mechanistic fingerprint.

A new compound, MAC13243, was suppressed at up to 16x MIC (MIC 8 ug/ml in the control strain) by overexpressed LolA, a periplasmic chaperone of the Lol lipoprotein-trafficking system, a suppressor not seen for any known antibiotic. Treating cells with MAC13243 caused 35S-labeled Braun's lipoprotein (Lpp) to accumulate in the inner membrane over 0-20 min (pulse-chase with sucrose gradient fractionation, TolC and YidC as membrane controls), as expected if LolA is inhibited. Saturation-transfer-difference NMR showed direct binding of MAC13243 to purified LolA, with Kd = 7.5 +/- 3 uM (Water-LOGSY). A structure-activity study of 18 analogs showed a strict requirement for a para halogen (Cl or Br) on the benzyl-thio ring and the triazine core, whereas the dimethoxyphenethyl part was dispensable (analog 19, MIC 8 ug/ml); the 4-chlorobenzyl thiol alone was inactive. MAC13243 was active against Gram-negative bacteria (E. coli MIC 16 ug/ml; P. aeruginosa PAO1 4 ug/ml) but not Gram-positive bacteria (>256 ug/ml), consistent with their lacking the Lol pathway, and showed no toxicity to HeLa cells or Candida. Its MIC was unaffected by AcrB overexpression (so it is a poor efflux substrate, unlike norfloxacin), and 16 ug/ml killed 12 of 16 clinical P. aeruginosa isolates, including multidrug-resistant strains. ## Significance in Introduction Context

- Gene dosage as an experimental tool in a haploid organism: overexpression (gain of copies) rescues phenotype, the converse of haploinsufficiency profiling in yeast.
- Shows how genetic suppression identifies the molecular target of a small molecule, linking classical suppressor analysis to drug discovery.
- Illustrates antibiotic resistance biology (efflux pumps, Gram-negative outer-membrane physiology).

## Future Research Directions

- Refine the MAC13243 pharmacophore and improve potency against P. aeruginosa and other Gram-negative pathogens.
- Extend high-copy suppression arrays to nonessential genes and to other bacteria to reveal fingerprints for new leads.
- Define how LolA binding by small molecules blocks lipoprotein trafficking structurally.
