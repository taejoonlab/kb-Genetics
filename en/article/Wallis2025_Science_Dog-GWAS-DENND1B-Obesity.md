# Canine genome-wide association study identifies DENND1B as an obesity gene in dogs and humans

## Citation (NLM)
Wallis NJ, McClellan A, Mörseburg A, Kentistou KA, Jamaluddin A, Dowsett GKC, et al. Canine genome-wide association study identifies DENND1B as an obesity gene in dogs and humans. Science. 2025;387(6738):eads2145. doi:10.1126/science.ads2145

**DOI:** [https://doi.org/10.1126/science.ads2145](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.ads2145)

---

## Summary

Obesity is highly heritable, but its genetic basis remains incompletely understood, and — the harder problem — **moving from a common genetic association to a mechanism has proven difficult**. Hypothalamic **leptin–melanocortin signalling** is the critical nexus of central energy balance control, and rare mutations in this pathway cause severe monogenic obesity, but the mechanisms behind common variants are usually opaque.

The authors ran a **canine GWAS for adiposity in Labrador retrievers**. The choice of species and breed is the strategic core. Dogs, like humans, live in an obesogenic environment and develop obesity; unlike humans, breeds are closed populations with long haplotype blocks, reduced genetic heterogeneity, and enriched risk alleles, so association mapping works with sample sizes orders of magnitude smaller. Labradors are additionally known for high food motivation and obesity risk.

The GWAS identified ***DENND1B***, and the authors then did what most GWAS cannot: they established a **mechanism**. DENND1B **facilitates clathrin-mediated endocytosis of MC4R**, the melanocortin-4 receptor — the single most important gene in human monogenic obesity, whose loss-of-function mutations are the commonest known cause. Endocytosis controls how much receptor is present at the cell surface and therefore how strongly the anorexigenic melanocortin signal is transduced. A gene that regulates MC4R trafficking is thus a direct modulator of the pathway, and the dog association pointed to a plausible new component of well-understood human biology.

The cross-species step completes the argument. The authors interrogated human data — GWAS for BMI in **806,834 participants** from GIANT, exome-wide association in **454,787 UK Biobank individuals**, and severe early-onset obesity cohorts including SCOOP — establishing that *DENND1B* affects **adiposity in humans as well as dogs**. The dog study was not a model of human obesity; it was a **discovery engine** whose finding was then validated in humans.

## Significance in Introduction Context

- The clearest possible demonstration of **why dogs are a powerful genetic model**: breed structure delivers mapping power that human cohorts require hundreds of thousands of individuals to match
- Shows the complete arc from **GWAS association to molecular mechanism to cross-species validation**, which most GWAS never complete
- Connects **common variant** genetics to a pathway defined by **rare monogenic mutations** (*MC4R*), illustrating the convergence theme
- Introduces **receptor trafficking and endocytosis** as a regulatory layer — the amount of receptor at the surface matters as much as the gene's expression
- Complements the Kooiker dog polymyositis study and the dog *de novo* mutation paper in this vault

## Future Research Directions

- Determine how *DENND1B* variants quantitatively alter MC4R surface levels and melanocortin signalling in hypothalamic neurons
- Test whether *DENND1B* is druggable as a means of enhancing MC4R signalling, given that MC4R agonists are already in clinical use
- Apply the canine discovery strategy to other complex traits where breed structure could deliver similar power
- Examine whether other regulators of MC4R trafficking harbour obesity-associated variation
- Assess whether *DENND1B* genotype predicts response to melanocortin-targeting obesity therapies in humans
