# Identifying genetic determinants of outer retinal function in mice using a large-scale gene-targeted screen

## Citation (NLM)
Wotton JM, Krebs MP, Sangermano R, Wong JK, Smith C, Willett AM, et al. Identifying genetic determinants of outer retinal function in mice using a large-scale gene-targeted screen. PLoS Genet. 2025;21(9):e1011886. doi:10.1371/journal.pgen.1011886

**DOI:** [https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1011886](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1011886)

---

## Summary

Vision starts in the retina, a precisely laminated neural tissue of neurons, glia, epithelium, and vasculature. Photoreceptors (rods can reliably detect a single photon) are supported by the retinal pigment epithelium. To understand how this tissue is built, maintained, and how it fails in inherited retinal disease, you need a complete list of the genes involved — and that list is far from complete.

This study is a **reverse-genetic, systematic, phenotype-driven screen** rather than a hypothesis-driven investigation of individual genes. The authors used **electroretinography (ERG)**, a non-invasive functional recording of the electrical response of outer retinal cell types to light, to phenotype **530 single-gene knockout mouse strains** generated by the **International Mouse Phenotyping Consortium (IMPC)** — about **2.5% of all protein-coding genes**. The IMPC's aim is to knock out every mouse gene and phenotype each line through a standard battery; this paper is what that infrastructure yields when a specialized assay is applied at scale.

**Thirty strains** showed significantly altered ERG amplitudes. Two of them — ***Cfap418*** and ***Syne2*** — were already known to cause outer retinal dysfunction, and the authors use them as **internal positive controls** validating the screen. That leaves **28 genes newly associated with altered retinal function**.

The most informative result is negative: **most of the 28 lacked a contemporaneous histopathological correlate**. The retina looked structurally normal at the time function was already measurably impaired. This makes a general point about phenotyping — a functional assay can detect a defect long before a structural one, and screens that rely on histology alone will systematically miss genes whose loss degrades performance before it degrades anatomy.

The study then closes the loop to human disease. The human orthologue of one of the 28 genes, ***FCHSD2***, was found to carry a **rare homozygous missense variant in a patient with retinal degeneration who had no molecular diagnosis**. A gene identified in an unbiased mouse screen provided the candidate that explained an undiagnosed human case — precisely the payoff that justifies large-scale model-organism phenotyping.

## Significance in Introduction Context

- A large-scale demonstration of **reverse genetics**: start from genes (knock them all out), then look for phenotypes — the mirror image of the forward-genetic screens covered elsewhere in the course
- Introduces the **IMPC** as a community resource and the general strategy of systematic, genome-wide knockout phenotyping
- The **function-before-structure** finding is a valuable methodological lesson about how assay choice determines what a screen can discover
- Shows the **mouse-to-human** translational pipeline concretely: mouse gene list → candidate → variant in an undiagnosed patient
- Reinforces the mouse model-organism lecture and connects it to the human inherited-disease lecture

## Future Research Directions

- Determine the cellular mechanism for each of the 28 new genes — which retinal cell type requires it, and does the ERG deficit reflect photoreceptor, bipolar, or RPE dysfunction?
- Follow the knockout lines longitudinally to test whether the functional deficits eventually produce structural degeneration, and on what timescale
- Screen human inherited retinal disease cohorts for variants in all 28 orthologues, not just *FCHSD2*
- Confirm causality of the *FCHSD2* variant functionally — model the specific missense change in mouse or in patient-derived retinal organoids
- Extend the ERG screen well beyond 2.5% of protein-coding genes; the hit rate here (30/530) suggests many more retinal-function genes remain unidentified
