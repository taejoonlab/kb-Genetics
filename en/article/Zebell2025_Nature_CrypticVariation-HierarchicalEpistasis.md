# Cryptic variation fuels plant phenotypic change through hierarchical epistasis

## Citation (NLM)
Zebell SG, Martí-Gómez C, Fitzgerald B, Cunha CP, Lach M, Seman BM, et al. Cryptic variation fuels plant phenotypic change through hierarchical epistasis. Nature. 2025;644(8078):984-993. doi:10.1038/s41586-025-09243-0

**DOI:** [https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09243-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09243-0)

---

## Summary

**Cryptic genetic variants** have little or no phenotypic effect on their own but are hypothesized to constitute a vast hidden reservoir of diversity that drives trait **evolvability** through **epistatic interactions** — becoming visible only in particular genetic backgrounds. This is a classical idea, recently reinvigorated by pan-genomics, which reveals pervasive variation within gene families, *cis*-regulatory regions, and regulatory networks. Testing it has been hard for three reasons: intractable genetics, limited allelic diversity, and inadequate phenotypic resolution. If cryptic variants have small effects by definition, detecting their interactions requires many alleles, many backgrounds, and precise measurement.

The authors overcame all three using engineered ***cis*-regulatory allelic series** in plants, guided by natural and engineered regulatory variation. CRISPR-based promoter editing can generate a graded series of alleles at a regulatory region — not just null and wild type, but many intermediate expression levels — and combining these across genes produces a designed matrix of genotypes spanning the interaction space. This converts epistasis from something inferred statistically into something constructed and measured.

The central finding is that the epistasis is **hierarchical** rather than pairwise and unstructured. Interactions are organized in a nested structure in which a variant's effect depends on the state of variants above it in the hierarchy, so cryptic variation is released in an ordered way: a background change at one level exposes variation at the next. That structure explains how a large reservoir of individually near-neutral variants can be maintained and then produce coherent, directional phenotypic change rather than noise — the hierarchy channels what would otherwise be a chaotic interaction landscape.

Because the system is built on *cis*-regulatory rather than coding variation, the work also connects to how regulatory evolution generates morphological change in plants and crops, where quantitative modulation of expression is the usual currency of trait improvement.

## Significance in Introduction Context

- Provides a mechanistic account of **epistasis** with real measured genotypes, complementing the Mackay pleiotropy/epistasis review in this vault
- Makes **cryptic genetic variation** and **evolvability** experimentally concrete rather than theoretical
- Demonstrates the modern use of **CRISPR-generated regulatory allelic series** — engineering the genotypes you need instead of hoping nature supplies them
- Explains how many small-effect variants can produce coordinated phenotypic change, which bears directly on complex-trait genetics
- Highly relevant to crop improvement, where tuning *cis*-regulatory alleles is now a mainstream breeding strategy

## Future Research Directions

- Determine how general the hierarchical structure is by applying the same design to other gene families and species
- Test whether the hierarchy is predictable from network topology, which would allow epistasis to be anticipated rather than measured exhaustively
- Ask whether natural populations harbour cryptic variation organized in the same hierarchy, or whether the structure is an artifact of engineered alleles
- Use the framework to design breeding strategies that deliberately release cryptic variation in a controlled direction
- Investigate whether environmental stress shifts the hierarchy, as classical canalization theory predicts
