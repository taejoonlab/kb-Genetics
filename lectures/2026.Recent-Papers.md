# BME333 / BIO333 Genetics (2026 Fall) — recent papers to read, by lecture

The recent papers you will read in this course. They were published between
September 2024 and September 2026 in the six journals below; one or two are assigned
per lecture, two or three for the four human-genetics lectures. Every citation has been
checked against PubMed.

Each paper carries three lines. **What to learn** is what you should be able to explain
once you have read it. **Check yourself** is a question to answer on your own — if you
cannot, read the paper again. **Vault note** links the bilingual study note for that
paper in this repository.

**Journals covered:** *Nature Genetics* · *Genome Biology* · *Genome Research* ·
*PLOS Genetics* · *Nature Reviews Genetics* · *GENETICS* (GSA)

**Marker:** ※ means the paper appeared in the first half of 2024, just outside the
two-year window, but nothing else covers that lecture as well.

## Part I · Transmission genetics

### 01 · [Evolutionary Ideas](../en/lectures/lec01_Evolutionary-Ideas.md)

- **Dönertaş & Partridge 2026** — Evolutionary genetics of ageing. *Nat Rev Genet* 2026;27:708-723. PMID: 42115756 · [10.1038/s41576-026-00959-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41576-026-00959-x)
  - **What to learn:** You should be able to explain ageing in terms of fitness, using the "shadow of selection" — the weakening of selection with age — together with antagonistic pleiotropy.
  - **Check yourself:** Is ageing an adaptation built by selection, or what is left where selection cannot reach — and can you answer using the 100-fold difference in maximum lifespan between species?
  - **Vault note:** [en](../en/review/Donertas2026_NatRevGenet_Ageing-EvolutionaryGenetics.md) · [ko](../ko/review/Donertas2026_NatRevGenet_Ageing-EvolutionaryGenetics.md)
- **Grah, Schmiedel & Bollback 2025** — Linking molecular mechanisms to their evolutionary consequences: a primer. *Genetics* 2025;229:iyae191. PMID: 39601269 · [10.1093/genetics/iyae191](https://doi.org/10.1093/genetics/iyae191)
  - **What to learn:** You should be able to draw the path by which a molecular mechanism is translated into a fitness difference between individuals, as a chain of variation, heredity and differential reproduction.
  - **Check yourself:** In the chain "mutation → phenotype → fitness", which step is hardest to measure in practice, and why?
  - **Vault note:** [en](../en/review/Grah2025_Genetics_MolecularMechanisms-EvolutionaryConsequences.md) · [ko](../ko/review/Grah2025_Genetics_MolecularMechanisms-EvolutionaryConsequences.md)

### 02 · [Tree of Life, Orthology & Paralogy](../en/lectures/lec02_Tree-of-Life-Orthology-Paralogy.md)

- **Fusca et al. 2025** — Phylogenomic timetree-calibrated speciation clocks for *Caenorhabditis* nematodes reveal slow but disproportionate accumulation of post-zygotic reproductive isolation. *PLoS Genet* 2025;21:e1011852. PMID: 40934278 · [10.1371/journal.pgen.1011852](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1011852)
  - **What to learn:** You should be able to explain how a species tree of 51 species is estimated, and why saturation of synonymous substitutions and codon bias must be corrected for when a molecular clock is calibrated without fossils.
  - **Check yourself:** Using the timetree figure, can you say why calibrating a molecular clock is difficult in a group with no fossil record?
  - **Vault note:** [en](../en/article/Fusca2025_PLoSGenet_Caenorhabditis-SpeciationClock.md) · [ko](../ko/article/Fusca2025_PLoSGenet_Caenorhabditis-SpeciationClock.md)
- **Cridland et al. 2025** — New perspectives on *Drosophila melanogaster* de novo gene origination revealed by investigation of ancient African genetic variation. *Genetics* 2025;230:iyaf044. PMID: 40106667 · [10.1093/genetics/iyaf044](https://doi.org/10.1093/genetics/iyaf044)
  - **What to learn:** You should be able to explain that judging homology is the starting point for telling orthologues from paralogues, using de novo genes with no homology to the ancestral transcriptome.
  - **Check yourself:** How do you prove that homology is absent, and can a negative BLAST result alone separate a de novo gene from a paralogue?
  - **Vault note:** [en](../en/article/Cridland2025_Genetics_Drosophila-DeNovoGenes.md) · [ko](../ko/article/Cridland2025_Genetics_Drosophila-DeNovoGenes.md)

### 03 · [Mendel's Principles of Heredity](../en/lectures/lec03_Mendel-Principles.md)

- **Liu et al. 2024** ※ — Reference genome sequence and population genomic analysis of peas provide insights into the genetic basis of Mendelian and other agronomic traits. *Nat Genet* 2024;56:1964-1974. PMID: 39103648 · [10.1038/s41588-024-01867-8](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-024-01867-8)
  - **What to learn:** You should be able to say which genes and haplotypes Mendel's alleles — stem length (Le/le), flower colour (A/a), cotyledon colour (I/i), seed shape (R/r), pod (P/p) — actually are, rather than knowing them only as textbook symbols.
  - **Check yourself:** Can you explain at the level of starch metabolism why the wrinkled seed (r) behaves as recessive?
  - *(follows naturally from `Kreplak2019_NatGenet_PeaGenome`, `Yang2022_NatGenet_PeaGenome2` and `Feng2025_Nature_MendelsMissingTraits` in the vault)*
  - **Vault note:** [en](../en/article/Liu2024_NatGenet_PeaGenome-MendelianTraits.md) · [ko](../ko/article/Liu2024_NatGenet_PeaGenome-MendelianTraits.md)
- **Vogan 2025** — Mendel's pea traits pinned down. *Nat Genet* 2025;57:1325. PMID: 40506535 · [10.1038/s41588-025-02242-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-025-02242-x)
  - **What to learn:** You should be able to summarise, in about one page, how the molecular identity of Mendel's traits came to be known.
  - **Check yourself:** Of Mendel's seven traits, can you say which have a confirmed causal gene and which do not yet?
  - **Vault note:** [en](../en/review/Vogan2025_NatGenet_ResearchHighlight-Pea-MendelTraits.md) · [ko](../ko/review/Vogan2025_NatGenet_ResearchHighlight-Pea-MendelTraits.md)

### 04 · [Extensions to Mendel's Laws](../en/lectures/lec04_Extensions-to-Mendel.md)

- **Wright et al. 2024** ※ — Guidance for estimating penetrance of monogenic disease-causing variants in population cohorts. *Nat Genet* 2024;56:1772-1779. PMID: 39075210 · [10.1038/s41588-024-01842-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-024-01842-3)
  - **What to learn:** You should be able to explain that penetrance is not a fixed property of an allele but an estimate that depends on how the sample was drawn (phenotype-first vs genotype-first).
  - **Check yourself:** For the very same variant, can you use ascertainment bias to explain why family-based penetrance estimates come out far higher than biobank-based ones?
  - **Vault note:** [en](../en/review/Wright2024_NatGenet_Penetrance-Estimation-Guidance.md) · [ko](../ko/review/Wright2024_NatGenet_Penetrance-Estimation-Guidance.md)
- **Tautz 2026** — Beyond Mendel: a call to revisit the genotype-phenotype map through new experimental paradigms. *Genetics* 2026;232:iyag024. PMID: 41701356 · [10.1093/genetics/iyag024](https://doi.org/10.1093/genetics/iyag024)
  - **What to learn:** You should be able to reconstruct the argument that the "one gene, one trait" scheme has limits, and that pleiotropy, epistasis and GxE are the default rather than the exception.
  - **Check yourself:** Are Mendel's laws wrong, or are they a special case that holds only under narrow conditions — and can you argue your answer?
  - **Vault note:** [en](../en/review/Tautz2026_Genetics_BeyondMendel-GenotypePhenotypeMap.md) · [ko](../ko/review/Tautz2026_Genetics_BeyondMendel-GenotypePhenotypeMap.md)

### 05 · [Chromosomes & Sex Determination](../en/lectures/lec05_Chromosomes-Sex-Determination.md)

- **Bracewell et al. 2024** — Sex and neo-sex chromosome evolution in beetles. *PLoS Genet* 2024;20:e1011477. PMID: 39585913 · [10.1371/journal.pgen.1011477](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgen.1011477)
  - **What to learn:** You should be able to explain the XY ↔ X0 transitions seen across the diverse beetle karyotypes, and the formation of neo-sex chromosomes by fusion with an autosome.
  - **Check yourself:** Can you predict what happens to the genes carried on a neo-X just after it forms, in terms of recombination suppression and degeneration?
  - **Vault note:** [en](../en/article/Bracewell2024_PLoSGenet_Beetle-NeoSexChromosome.md) · [ko](../ko/article/Bracewell2024_PLoSGenet_Beetle-NeoSexChromosome.md)
- **Bruhn-Olszewska et al. 2025** — The effects of loss of Y chromosome on male health. *Nat Rev Genet* 2025;26:320-335. PMID: 39743536 · [10.1038/s41576-024-00805-y](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41576-024-00805-y)
  - **What to learn:** You should be able to connect karyotype and aneuploidy to human disease through mosaic loss of the Y chromosome in somatic cells, and to say that the Y has functions beyond sex determination.
  - **Check yourself:** Can you explain why the most common acquired mutation in men is loss of Y rather than loss of X?
  - **Vault note:** [en](../en/review/BruhnOlszewska2025_NatRevGenet_LossOfY-MaleHealth.md) · [ko](../ko/review/BruhnOlszewska2025_NatRevGenet_LossOfY-MaleHealth.md)

### 06 · [Central Dogma, DNA Structure & Replication](../en/lectures/lec06_Central-Dogma-DNA-Structure-Replication.md)

- **Carrington et al. 2025** — Most human DNA replication initiation is dispersed throughout the genome with only a minority within previously identified initiation zones. *Genome Biol* 2025;26:122. PMID: 40346587 · [10.1186/s13059-025-03591-w](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-025-03591-w)
  - **What to learn:** You should be able to use the data to explain that the "origin" of the textbooks is, in the human genome, not a small set of fixed sites but something dispersed throughout.
  - **Check yourself:** How far can the single-oriC model of *E. coli* account for dispersed replication initiation in the human genome?
  - **Vault note:** [en](../en/article/Carrington2025_GenomeBiol_Human-ReplicationInitiation-SingleMolecule.md) · [ko](../ko/article/Carrington2025_GenomeBiol_Human-ReplicationInitiation-SingleMolecule.md)

  - **What to learn:** You should be able to mark on the central dogma which step — transcription, splicing, mRNA stability or translation — a synonymous mutation acts on, even though it leaves the protein sequence unchanged.
  - **Check yourself:** Are silent mutations really silent — can you answer by pointing to where they act at each step of the central dogma?
  - **Vault note:** [en](../en/review/Zhang2025_NatRevGenet_SynonymousMutations.md) · [ko](../ko/review/Zhang2025_NatRevGenet_SynonymousMutations.md)

### 07 · [Recombination & Linkage](../en/lectures/lec07_Recombination-Linkage.md)

  - **What to learn:** You should be able to explain why meiotic recombination concentrates in short hotspots, in terms of determinants such as PRDM9 and hypotheses about their evolutionary origin.
  - **Check yourself:** Can you use the distribution of hotspots to explain why genetic map distance and physical distance disagree in two-point and three-point mapping, which assume uniform recombination?

  - **What to learn:** You should be able to explain how LD information is converted into a recombination map, using the estimated termite recombination rate (~1 cM/Mb) as the worked case.

### 08 · [Mutation & Copy Number Variation](../en/lectures/lec08_Mutation-CNV.md)

- **Zhang et al. 2025** — Determinants of de novo mutations in extended pedigrees of 43 dog breeds. *Genome Biol* 2025;26:305. PMID: 40999484 · [10.1186/s13059-025-03804-2](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-025-03804-2)
  - **What to learn:** You should be able to explain how the per-generation, per-base mutation rate (~4.9×10⁻⁹), the paternal age effect and the CpG effect are quantified from sequencing 390 trios — that is, how a mutation rate is measured at all.
  - **Check yourself:** Can you link recombination to the finding that CpG islands show a high mutation rate in dogs, which lack PRDM9?

  - **What to learn:** You should be able to state, with evidence, the view of transposable elements as a source of variation and innovation rather than merely parasitic DNA.

  - **Vault note:** [en](../en/review/Feschotte2026_NatRevGenet_TransposableElements-Innovation.md) · [ko](../ko/review/Feschotte2026_NatRevGenet_TransposableElements-Innovation.md)

  - **Check yourself:** Can you pair each type of rearrangement with its formation mechanism and the clinical outcome that follows?

  - **What to learn:** You should be able to explain the design principles, and the cautions in interpretation, that apply when a large pooled CRISPR screen is run in vivo rather than in cultured cells.
  - **Check yourself:** Can you set the design logic of a forward screen and a reverse perturbation screen side by side and say what is the same and what differs?

# Appendix C · Methods — what to learn from the seven Method boxes

In [Syllabus v3](2026.BME333-BIO333-Syllabus-v3.md), each Monday lecture in W9–W15 ends with a single **10-minute Method box**.
All seven boxes sit where they open the presentation of the following Wednesday.

The seven techniques covered here are the ones **without which you cannot read the figures of the Wednesday paper**.
That is the only selection criterion.

Each box is **one page, one figure, no equations, 10 minutes**. If you already know the technique, treat that box as revision and move on.

---

## C-1. What to learn from each box

### Box 1 · W9 Mon (lec13) — from reads to a variant table, and why long reads

- **What to learn:**
  - You should be able to draw the FASTQ → BAM → VCF flow in one figure
  - You should be able to say what the DP/GQ/AF columns of a VCF each mean
  - You should be able to explain that a filter threshold is a judgement, not a fact
  - You should be able to say what it means for a long read to span a repeat, and how T2T and pangenome approaches replace the fiction of "one reference genome"
- **Unlocks:** S1 Feltes · S5 Zhang · S6 Porubsky, Del Gobbo — effectively every session
- **Further reading:** Koboldt 2020, *Genome Med* 12:91 (PMID 33106175, open access) ·
  Logsdon 2020, *Nat Rev Genet* 21:597-614 (PMID 32504078)

### Box 2 · W10 Mon (lec15) — the gene-editing toolbox on one page

- **What to learn:**
  - You should be able to lay out KO / KI / dCas9 (CRISPRi/a) / base editor / prime editor in a single table, and distinguish what each one can change from what it cannot
  - You should be able to say that off-target activity is a measured quantity, not "zero", and how it is measured
- **Unlocks:** S2 Simkhada, Zillich · S4 Wotton · and the W16 Mon Gene Therapy lecture
- **Further reading:** Anzalone 2020, *Nat Biotechnol* 38:824-844 (PMID 32572269) — it places all four tools
  side by side as schematics in the same format, so one table can be lifted straight from it

### Box 3 · W11 Mon (lec27 Bacterial Genetics Today) — how to trace a lineage

- **What to learn:**
  - You should be able to distinguish the three ways of breaking a population into trackable clones — **DNA barcodes** in microbial culture, **Cre/lox and multicolour labelling** in animals, and **reading somatic mutations as natural barcodes**
  - You should be able to explain that the same recombinase is used both to switch a gene off and to label a cell permanently so its descendants can be followed
  - You should be able to read what a clone size distribution tells you
- **Unlocks:** S3 Guan 2025 (3D map of cell lineage) · and the discussion of clonal expansion in W14 Cancer
- **Further reading:** no separate primer is needed; the lecture itself is enough. Barcode lineage tracing follows
  naturally from the experimental evolution part of lec27.

### Box 4 · W12 Mon (lec19) — how to read a UMAP

- **What to learn:**
  - You should be able to say that the raw data is an expression matrix (cells × gene counts), and that **one dot on a UMAP is one cell**
  - You should be able to explain that the axes have no units and that the distance between clusters is not a real distance
  - You should be able to point out the risk of circular reasoning in labelling cell types by marker genes
- **Unlocks:** S3 Guan · and, from the Monday lectures, Chen 2025 (lec21) and Poeschla 2026 (lec23).
  **Most of the figures in the second half of the term depend on this** — of the seven boxes, this one has the widest reach.
- **Further reading:** Heumos 2023, *Nat Rev Genet* 24:550-572 (PMID 37002403) — built around boxes and diagrams, the first half is enough ·
  Luecken 2019, *Mol Syst Biol* 15:e8746 (PMID 31217225) — open access plus hands-on code, can be set as an assignment

### Box 5 · W13 Mon (lec21) — knockdown vs knockout, and genetic compensation

- **What to learn:**
  - You should be able to explain that a morpholino is transient and prone to off-target effects, and that maternal mRNA contribution can mask the phenotype
  - You should be able to say what genetic compensation is (a paralogue is upregulated in the mutant and the phenotype disappears)
  - You should be able to judge what settles the question when the two methods give opposite conclusions
- **Unlocks:** S2 Zillich (contrast with organoids) · S4 Wotton (interpreting a knockout screen)
- **Further reading:** no separate primer is needed — Flannery 2026, *PLoS Genet* 22:e1011987
  (PMID 41533692), already assigned in the main text, is exactly this case, so read the Monday lecture material as it stands.

### Box 6 · W14 Mon (lec23) — VAF and duplex sequencing

- **What to learn:**
  - You should be able to state the definition of VAF (variant allele fraction)
  - You should be able to explain the wall set by a sequencing error rate of ~10⁻³, and the principle by which a double-stranded consensus erases that error
  - You should be able to read the relationship between **VAF and clone size**
  - You should be able to point to the measurement technology that made the conclusion "even normal tissue carries competing mutant clones" possible
- **Unlocks:** S6 Porubsky (why a variant at VAF 0.15 is reclassified as postzygotic) ·
  Lawson 2025 and Oliver 2025 from the Monday lectures
- **Further reading:** Salk 2018, *Nat Rev Genet* 19:269-285 (PMID 29576615) — it builds error rate → UMI → consensus → duplex
  up step by step in figures

### Box 7 · W15 Mon (lec12 Complex Traits & QTL) — credible sets and PRS

- **What to learn:**
  - You should be able to say that what fine-mapping actually returns is not a gene but a **credible set**
  - You should be able to explain in one sentence that a PRS is a sum of GWAS weights (clumping + threshold)
  - You should be able to carry out the **conversion from relative to absolute risk** yourself (does "3.5-fold in the top 1%" mean 2% → 7%, or 0.02% → 0.07%?)
  - You should be able to follow the path from the LOD interval of a QTL to the credible set of a GWAS
- **Unlocks:** **the whole of the S7 GWAS session** (Hatzikotoulas 2025 · Nunes 2025 · Jee 2025) ·
  S5 Wallis (dog-breed GWAS contrasted with human sample sizes)
- **Further reading:** Schaid 2018, *Nat Rev Genet* 19:491-504 (PMID 29844615) — the standard introduction to credible sets ·
  Choi 2020, *Nat Protoc* 15:2759-2772 (PMID 32709988) — a step-by-step PRS tutorial
